Reference

Football Analytics Glossary | Elite Football Predictor

The language of football analytics, defined without the jargon.

A working glossary of the terms used across the site. Definitions are written for readers who want to understand the idea, not memorise a slogan. Where a term is contested or provider-specific, the definition says so.

Use the jump links to move between groups, or search the page with your browser. Each entry links to a longer guide where one exists.

A to D E to P Q to Z

A to D

Expected goals (xG)
A shot-quality model that estimates the probability that a given shot becomes a goal, then sums those probabilities across a match or season. xG describes the chances a team created, not the goals it actually scored. Read the full xG guide.
Expected goals against (xGA)
The xG a team allows its opponents to accumulate. It measures the quality of chances conceded before the goalkeeper acts. Read the xGA guide.
Expected goals on target (xGOT)
A post-shot model that accounts for where a shot on target crosses the goal line, not only where it was struck. It is useful for evaluating finishing and goalkeeper shot-stopping.
Post-shot xG
The expected-goal value of a shot after it has been taken, including placement and trajectory. Comparing goals conceded with post-shot xG helps separate a goalkeeper's saves from the defence in front of him.
Expected threats (xT)
A possession-value model that estimates how much a pass or carry increases the chance of a future goal, based on the start and end location of the ball. It measures progression rather than the shot itself. Read the xT guide.
Expected assists (xA)
The xG value of the shots that follow a player's passes. It credits chance creation rather than the final pass alone, and it is not a complete measure of creativity.
Expected goal difference (xGD)
A team's xG for minus xG against. It is a process measure, not a league table. Compare it with actual goal difference to see which teams may be overperforming or underperforming. See goal difference vs xG difference.
Goal difference (GD)
Goals scored minus goals conceded. It is a real outcome and a tiebreaker in many leagues, but it is noisy over short samples because a single match can move it sharply.
Shot map
A visual plot of every shot in a match or season, usually sized or coloured by xG. It shows whether a team creates central, close-range chances or relies on low-value attempts. See data visualisation.
Progressive pass
A pass that moves the ball substantially closer to the opponent's goal, usually measured against a threshold based on distance and direction. Definitions vary by provider, so compare like with like.
Final third
The attacking third of the pitch. Entries into the final third are a useful measure of progression, but they say little about the quality of the chance eventually created.
Box entry
A pass, carry, or cross that moves the ball into the penalty area. Box entries correlate with chance creation, but a high count can hide poor shot quality.

E to P

PPDA (passes allowed per defensive action)
A pressing metric that divides the number of passes an opponent completes in a defined zone by the number of defensive actions the pressing team makes. Lower values generally mean more aggressive pressing. Read PPDA explained.
Pressing
The coordinated attempt to win the ball back quickly by closing down opponents. Effective pressing needs triggers, compactness, and cover behind the press, not just effort.
High press
A defensive approach that starts near the opponent's penalty area, aiming to force turnovers in dangerous areas. It carries a physical cost and leaves space behind if the first line is beaten.
Low block
A deep, compact defensive shape that concedes territory and protects the penalty area. It can be effective against possession-heavy teams but invites sustained pressure.
High defensive line
A defensive line positioned far from its own goal. It compresses space and supports pressing, but it is vulnerable to through balls and fast runners.
Transition
The moment a team wins or loses possession. Transition quality often decides matches because both teams are temporarily disorganised.
Game state
The score, time, and match situation that shapes how a team behaves. A side leading 1-0 may press less and defend deeper, which changes possession and shot numbers. See possession data.
Set piece
A dead-ball situation such as a corner, free kick, throw-in, or penalty. Set-piece quality is partly repeatable and partly dependent on the opponent's defensive structure. Read set pieces in analysis.
Clean sheet
A match in which a team concedes no goals. It is a useful outcome but a blunt measure of defensive quality because it depends heavily on goalkeeper performance and opponent finishing.
Both teams to score (BTTS)
A market or summary measure that asks whether each side scores at least once. The model on this site derives it from the scoreline grid. It is an estimate, not a recommendation.
Over/under goals
A total-goals line such as 2.5. Over 2.5 means three or more goals; under 2.5 means two or fewer. The half-goal line avoids a push. See the model.
Double chance
A combined outcome covering two of the three 1X2 results: home or draw, draw or away, or home or away. It raises the probability of the combined outcome but usually lowers the price.
Draw no bet (DNB)
A market in which the stake is returned if the match is drawn. It removes the draw from the result but does not remove the risk of losing. It is a betting term, not a recommendation.
Asian handicap
A handicap market that gives one side a goal start or deficit, often with quarter-goal lines that split the stake. It is used to balance prices between uneven teams.

Q to Z

Implied probability
The probability suggested by a set of odds, calculated before removing the bookmaker's margin. Decimal odds of 2.50 imply 40%. Read implied probability and expected value.
Overround
The amount by which the implied probabilities of all outcomes in a market exceed 100%. It is the bookmaker's built-in margin. A larger overround makes the market more expensive for the customer.
Market price
The odds available for an outcome at a given moment. A price reflects the market's assessment plus its margin; it is not a statement of truth. Read how to read odds.
Expected value (EV)
The long-run average result of a repeated decision, calculated by weighting each outcome by its probability. A positive EV estimate is not a guarantee, and a single result says little about whether the estimate was good.
Poisson distribution
A probability model for counts, often used for goals. It assumes a steady average rate and independence between events. Real matches violate both assumptions in small ways. Read the Poisson guide.
Sample size
The number of observations behind a statistic. Small samples are noisy, so a five-match xG run is weaker evidence than a twenty-match run. See form streaks.
Regression to the mean
The tendency of extreme results to move closer to average over time. A team scoring on every rare chance is likely to cool down; a team missing everything is likely to improve. It is a statistical effect, not a moral judgement.
Calibration
How closely predicted probabilities match observed frequencies. If forecasts labelled 60% happen about 60% of the time over a large sample, the forecasts are well calibrated. Read tracking prediction accuracy.
Brier score
A scoring rule for probabilistic forecasts. It is the average squared difference between the predicted probability and the actual outcome. Lower is better. It rewards both confidence and correctness.
Log loss
A scoring rule that penalises confident wrong predictions heavily. It is useful for comparing probability models, but it is less intuitive than accuracy or Brier score.
Closing line
The final price before an event starts. It is often used as a benchmark because it incorporates late information. Beating the closing line is not proof of skill over a small sample. See odds movement.
Opening line
The first price offered when a market opens. Comparing it with the closing line shows how information and money moved the market.
Bankroll
The amount a person has set aside for betting. This site does not recommend a staking system. The concept matters because losing a large share of a bankroll can make recovery mathematically harder. Read bankroll basics.
Return on investment (ROI)
Profit or loss divided by the amount staked, usually expressed as a percentage. Small samples produce wild ROI figures, and a positive ROI over a short run is not evidence of a sustainable edge. This site does not publish an ROI record.
Home advantage
The performance benefit associated with playing at home, driven by crowd effects, travel, familiarity, and referee tendencies. It is not a fixed number and varies by club, competition, and context. Read home advantage.
Possession percentage
The share of time or passes a team controls the ball. It is not the same as control of the match. A team can have less possession and create better chances. See possession data.
Fixture congestion
A period with matches close together, reducing recovery time and increasing rotation. It is a context variable, not a guarantee of a poor performance.
Squad depth
The quality and fit of options beyond the first eleven. Depth matters during injuries, suspensions, and congestion. Read squad depth.

How to use the glossary

Definitions are starting points, not substitutes for context. A metric can be useful in one question and misleading in another. For example, xG is a good summary of chance quality but a poor description of defensive structure; PPDA is a useful pressing indicator but a poor measure of pressing effectiveness on its own.

If a term is missing, send a suggestion through the contact page. If a definition is unclear or contested, tell us and include a source. We update entries when the explanation can be made more accurate.
