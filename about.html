About

About Elite Football Predictor

An independent project about football data, clear reasoning, and the limits of prediction.

Elite Football Predictor is an independent football analytics and education site. It exists to explain how match data is collected, modelled, and misread, and to show a transparent alternative to tipster culture.

Why this site exists

Football analysis is full of confident claims. A team is "due" a win. A price is "too good to miss". A model is described as intelligent and therefore assumed to be objective. Most of that language hides the same problem: uncertainty is being sold as certainty.

This site takes the opposite approach. We publish explanations, not tips. We show the arithmetic behind a claim. We label illustrative numbers. We explain what a model cannot see, and we do not pretend that a probability distribution is a promise.

The project began in 2026 as a rewrite of a single-page football predictor. The original page mixed valid questions about chance quality with invalid HTML, AMP components on a non-AMP page, and language that promised more than any model can deliver. The rebuild separates the tool from the education, removes bookmaker promotion, and treats readers as adults who can handle nuance.

Who writes the site

Nyasha is the founder and editor. He writes about football data, tactical structure, and the psychology of prediction. His interest is in the gap between what a number measures and what people assume it measures: why xG is not a scoreline forecast, why possession is not control, and why a well-calibrated model can still lose a single match.

He does not claim a professional scouting licence, a bookmaker partnership, or a verified betting record. The site does not sell tips, subscriptions, or affiliate offers. The goal is to make analytical ideas legible, not to build a personality cult around a forecast.

What we publish

  • Explainers on xG, xGA, expected threats, PPDA, post-shot xG, and other commonly misused metrics.
  • Tactical notes on formations, pressing, set pieces, goalkeeping, and matchup structure.
  • League guides that describe styles, data quality, and competition context rather than pretending to know current form from memory.
  • Betting education about probability, market prices, bias, and risk. We do not publish selections, staking plans, or profit claims.
  • Beginner guides that define terms and walk through a worked example without assuming a statistics background.

How we work

Every article follows a repeatable structure. We define the idea, explain the mechanism, show a worked example with clearly labelled illustrative numbers, list common mistakes, and state the limits. The model page follows the same principle: the inputs, the arithmetic, the scoreline grid, and the assumptions are all visible.

We prefer primary concepts over recycled conclusions. If an article discusses a metric, it explains what the metric counts, what it ignores, and what kind of error it invites. If an article uses a numeric example, the arithmetic is shown so a reader can check it.

We use software for calculations, editing, and site construction. We do not present automated text as a substitute for judgement. Published analysis is reviewed for clarity, consistency, and policy safety before it goes live.

Editorial standards

Our full standards are on the editorial policy page. In short: original writing, no scraped articles, no invented records, no hidden affiliate relationships, no gambling promotion, and no claims that a model can guarantee an outcome.

We correct errors when we find them. If a correction changes the meaning of an article, we note it on the page rather than quietly editing the past. The "last updated" date on each article is there to make the revision history visible.

What we do not do

  • We do not accept bets, hold funds, or process payments.
  • We do not link to bookmakers, affiliate offers, or tipster services.
  • We do not publish certainties, near-certainties, or claims of a repeatable winning method.
  • We do not scrape live statistics and present them as our own dataset.
  • We do not offer personal financial, legal, or medical advice.
