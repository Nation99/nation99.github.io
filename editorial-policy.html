Editorial Policy and Standards

How the site is written, reviewed, corrected, and kept independent.

Last updated 18 November 2026

This page explains how we decide what to publish, how we use data, how the model is presented, and how readers can hold us to a consistent standard.

Our purpose

We publish football analytics education. The site is not a tipping service, a gambling operator, or a news wire. Our job is to explain ideas clearly enough that a reader can evaluate them, including the parts that are uncertain or contested.

Authorship and review

Articles are written and edited by Nyasha, the founder and editor. Each article carries a byline, a publish date, a last updated date, and an estimated reading time. We do not use fake author names or invented credentials.

We use software for calculations, drafting, editing, and site construction. Published analysis is reviewed by a human before it goes live. Automated output is never presented as a substitute for judgement, and the model is described as a statistical tool rather than an intelligence or an authority.

How we use data

We prefer conceptual explanations and transparent arithmetic over a blizzard of numbers. When we use a numeric example, it is either a simple calculation that a reader can reproduce or a clearly labelled illustrative scenario using hypothetical clubs such as Club A and Club B.

We do not scrape articles or statistics and republish them as original work. We do not invent win rates, returns, track records, or "proof" screenshots. If a claim depends on a live dataset, we say so and point readers toward the kind of source they should check rather than pretending that our page is the source of truth.

How we make predictions

We do not publish match selections. The predictor is an educational model that produces estimates. Its method is visible on the predictor page:

  1. Combine each side's attacking and defensive averages into an expected-goals pair.
  2. Apply small, explicit adjustments for venue, form, availability, and rest.
  3. Use two Poisson distributions to generate a scoreline grid.
  4. Sum the grid into home, draw, away, over/under, and both-teams-to-score estimates.
  5. Label the output as an estimate and list the assumptions it cannot test.

We do not claim that this method is novel, complete, or superior to professional models. It is a teaching model. Its value is that a reader can see every step and argue with it.

Original content and sourcing

Every article is written for this site. We do not copy text from other publications. When an idea has a well-known origin, such as the Poisson model or expected goals, we explain the concept in our own words and focus on application and limitations rather than reproducing a paper.

We encourage readers to check primary sources, including match data providers, competition rules, and published research. We do not claim to have access to proprietary datasets unless we explicitly say so.

Corrections and updates

If you find a factual error, a broken link, a misleading example, or a policy problem, please contact us. We review corrections and update the article when a change is warranted. When an update changes the substance of a piece, we change the "last updated" date and, where useful, add a short note explaining what changed.

We do not silently rewrite an article to win an argument. The goal is accuracy, not the appearance of consistency.

Advertising and independence

The site may display advertising to fund hosting and editing. Advertisements are labelled "Advertisement" and kept separate from editorial content. Advertisers do not review articles, choose topics, or receive approval over what we write. We do not accept paid links, sponsored articles disguised as editorial, or affiliate arrangements with bookmakers.

If advertising is present, it does not change our analysis. If an advertisement is misleading or incompatible with our standards, we can remove the placement or the partner.

Responsible language

We avoid language that promises outcomes. We do not use terms that suggest certainty, a promised result, or easy returns. We describe outputs as estimates, probabilities, or analytical insights. We remind readers that football is uncertain and that a model can be wrong even when its reasoning is sound.

We do not publish staking plans, chase losses, or imply that a statistical edge removes the risk of financial loss. Our betting education content explains concepts such as implied probability, overround, and variance; it does not tell readers what to do.

What we will not publish

  • Bookmaker promotions, affiliate offers, or bonus code pages.
  • Match-fixing claims or unverified allegations against named individuals.
  • Medical, legal, or financial advice.
  • Content that targets children or encourages underage gambling.
  • Scraped articles, fabricated quotes, or invented statistics.
  • Personal data about private individuals without a lawful basis.

Contact

Editorial questions and corrections: editor@elitefootballpredictor.com. You can also use the contact form. For privacy requests, please include "Privacy request" in the subject line.
