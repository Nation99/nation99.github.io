Responsible Gambling

Help, warning signs, and practical steps if gambling is causing harm.

Last updated 18 November 2026

This site is football analytics education, not a betting service. We do not accept bets, process payments, or link to bookmakers. If gambling is affecting your life, please stop and use the independent support below.

18+ only

The site is intended for adults aged 18 or over. It is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly collect data from children. If you are under 18, please leave the site.

Our position

Football analysis can be interesting without being a reason to gamble. We publish education about probability, uncertainty, and market structure because those ideas are useful for understanding football. We do not publish selections, staking plans, or claims that a model can produce reliable income.

Betting markets contain a built-in margin. Over many repeated bets, that margin makes losing the most likely outcome for most participants, even when individual analysis is sound. No amount of modelling removes the risk of financial loss.

Warning signs

Gambling may be becoming harmful if you recognise any of the following:

  • Spending more money or time than you intended.
  • Chasing losses with larger or more frequent bets.
  • Hiding gambling from family, friends, or work.
  • Borrowing money, selling possessions, or missing bills to gamble.
  • Feeling anxious, irritable, or low when you cannot gamble.
  • Neglecting sleep, work, studies, relationships, or health.
  • Believing that a system, model, or "sure thing" will recover your losses.
  • Thinking about gambling constantly, even when you want to stop.

If any of these apply, it is not a character failure. It is a signal to get support.

Independent support organisations

BeGambleAware

Free, confidential information, advice, and support for people affected by gambling in the UK.

begambleaware.org

GambleAware

Independent charity funding research, education, and treatment for gambling harm in Great Britain.

gambleaware.org

Gamblers Anonymous

Fellowship-based support groups for people who want to stop gambling, with meetings in many countries.

gamblersanonymous.org

GamCare

Free helpline, live chat, and counselling for anyone affected by gambling in the UK.

gamcare.org.uk

GAMSTOP

A free UK self-exclusion scheme that blocks access to licensed online gambling sites for a chosen period.

gamstop.co.uk

National Gambling Helpline

Free, confidential support in the UK, available every day. Call 0808 8020 133 or visit the helpline site.

gamcare.org.uk/get-support

Practical steps

  1. Pause. Stop gambling for a day, a week, or longer. A pause makes it easier to see the pattern.
  2. Remove access. Delete gambling apps, block sites, and consider a self-exclusion scheme such as GAMSTOP in the UK.
  3. Tell someone. Speak to a trusted friend, family member, or support worker. Secrecy makes harm worse.
  4. Protect your money. Ask your bank about gambling blocks, lower limits, or a trusted person helping with finances.
  5. Get professional support. Contact one of the organisations above. Counselling and peer support are free and confidential in many countries.
  6. Treat relapses as information. A relapse does not mean you have failed. It means the support plan needs adjusting.

If you are outside the UK

Support services vary by country. Search for a national gambling helpline or a recognised counselling service in your region. Gamblers Anonymous has meetings in many countries. If you are in immediate danger, contact local emergency services.

For family and friends

Gambling harm affects more than the person gambling. Partners, family members, and friends can also get confidential support from BeGambleAware and GamCare. You do not have to manage alone, and you are not responsible for fixing another adult's behaviour.
